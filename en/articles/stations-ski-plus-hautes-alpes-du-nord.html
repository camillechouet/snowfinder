The 10 ski areas where you ski the highest in the French Northern Alps

✍️ SnowFinder Editorial Team📅 23 November 2026⏱ 10 min

Altitude is the best indicator of lasting snow cover. Here are the ski areas where you ski the highest in the French Northern Alps, ranked by their highest point. A resort linked to others is counted with its ski area. We reviewed the 85 resorts in our database in the French Northern Alps that qualify for this ranking.

Resorts linked together form a single ski area: we group them and rank the area, represented by its highest resort. The other resorts in the area are mentioned in its description.

Key points: Chamonix is the ski area where you ski the highest (3,842 m), ahead of Les Deux Alpes (3,568 m) · in 10th place, La Norma, reaches 2,750 m.

The ranking: 10 ski areas

1Chamonix

3,842 m
16434416

The ski area peaks at 3,842 m (lowest point: 1,035 m), with 110 km of runs. Adult day lift pass: €77.

Chamonix-Mont-Blanc is the world capital of mountaineering, nestled at 1,035 m at the foot of Mont Blanc (4,808 m). Its 170 km ski area is spread across Les Grands Montets, Le Brévent and La Flégère. The Vallée Blanche (24 km of off-piste) is the most legendary glacier descent in the world. A demanding destination, wilder than the big purpose-built resorts.

2Les Deux Alpes

3,568 m
17452212

The ski area peaks at 3,568 m (lowest point: 1,650 m), with 220 km of runs. Adult day lift pass: €68.

Les Deux Alpes is an international resort in the Oisans, Isère, at 1,650 m in the Écrins massif, with one of the highest skiable glaciers in Europe (3,568 m). Its 220 km of marked runs, 75% of them above 2,000 m, guarantee good snow from December to May. The Jandri cable car, opened in December 2024, reaches 3,200 m in 17 minutes. A very lively resort, with around 80 bars and restaurants, a renowned snowpark and the Famille Plus label.

3Tignes – Val d'Isère

3,456 m
6382016

This ski area links several resorts and peaks at 3,456 m, at Tignes. Adult day lift pass at Tignes: €70. It also includes Val d'Isère.

Spotlight on Tignes: Tignes was built in the 1950s after the Chevril dam was filled. Together with Val d'Isère, it forms the Tignes-Val d'Isère ski area (300 km). Its Grande Motte glacier (3,456 m), open almost all year round, makes it a world reference. A car-free resort, very popular with snowboarders and freeriders, with one of Europe's best snowparks.

4Alpe d'Huez

3,330 m
19171610

The ski area peaks at 3,330 m (lowest point: 1,135 m), with 250 km of runs. Adult day lift pass: €68.

Alpe d'Huez is a legendary resort at 1,860 m in the Grandes Rousses massif, famous for its exceptional sunshine (300 days a year) and its Sarenne run (16 km, the longest black run in Europe). Its 250 km of runs climb to 3,330 m. The drive up from Bourg-d'Oisans is a legendary col on the Tour de France.

5Paradiski

3,250 m
9783521

This ski area links several resorts and peaks at 3,250 m, at La Plagne. Adult day lift pass at La Plagne: €72. It also includes Montchavin-Les Coches, Champagny-en-Vanoise, Les Arcs and Peisey-Vallandry.

Spotlight on La Plagne: La Plagne is one of the most visited ski areas in the world, spread across 10 villages between 1,250 and 2,100 m. Together with Les Arcs, it forms Paradiski (425 km). It hosted the Olympic bobsleigh events at the 1992 Games and the track is still open to the public. Very well suited to families, it offers a wide variety of runs and activities.

6Les Trois Vallées

3,230 m
1235308

This ski area links several resorts and peaks at 3,230 m, at Val Thorens. Adult day lift pass at Val Thorens: €74. It also includes Orelle, Méribel, Les Menuires, Courchevel, La Tania and Saint-Martin-de-Belleville.

Spotlight on Val Thorens: Val Thorens is the highest resort in Europe at 2,300 m, at the summit of the 3 Vallées (600 km). This altitude guarantees quality snow from November to May. A modern, lively and very busy resort, it is popular with young skiers thanks to its many bars and clubs at the foot of the runs. Its own ski area has 85 runs over 150 km.

7Bonneval-sur-Arc

3,000 m
61073

The ski area peaks at 3,000 m (lowest point: 1,800 m), with 32 km of runs. Adult day lift pass: €38.

Bonneval-sur-Arc is the most beautiful village in Haute-Maurienne, ranked among the Plus Beaux Villages de France, at 1,800 m on the edge of the Vanoise National Park. Its small 25 km ski area is intimate and unspoilt, offering authentic skiing in an exceptional setting. No concrete mars its grey stone chalets.

8La Rosière

2,800 m
1015113

The ski area peaks at 2,800 m (lowest point: 1,850 m), with 65 km of runs. Adult day lift pass: €59.

La Rosière is a resort at 1,850 m, facing due south and very sunny throughout the day: an ideal setting for enjoying the runs and terraces in winter, with uninterrupted views of Mont Blanc. Together with La Thuile on the Italian side, it forms the Espace San Bernardo: 156 km of runs between France and Italy, from 1850 to 2,800 m, with 83 runs (8 green, 25 blue, 35 red, 15 black), 38 lifts and 4 snowparks. The ski area benefits from good snow reliability throughout the winter: La Rosière in particular benefits from the Atlantic weather systems coming from the west, which often bring heavier snowfall here than elsewhere in the valley. The Italian border can be crossed on skis to reach La Thuile: you can start your day in France, cross into Italy for a pizza or a pasta dish at lunch, then return to La Rosière in the afternoon. Families and intermediate skiers enjoy wide, accessible blue runs, while more experienced skiers have more technical sectors and a freeride area, notably the Mont-Valaisan zone, to ski on ungroomed but secured terrain.

The ski area peaks at 2,800 m (lowest point: 1,400 m), with 125 km of runs. Adult day lift pass: €50.

Val Cenis is a Haute-Maurienne resort at 1,400 m, grouping several villages including Lanslebourg and Lanslevillard. Its 125 km ski area is one of the sunniest in Savoie. Gateway to the Vanoise and its national park, it offers varied skiing in a magnificent high-mountain setting.

What this ranking reveals

Chamonix tops this ranking at 3,842 m, 1,092 m more than the 10th-placed La Norma (2,750 m). 7 of the 10 ski areas exceed 3,000 m.

Why altitude matters: on average, the temperature drops by about 0.6 °C every 100 metres. The higher a ski area goes, the better its chances of keeping snow for longer, especially at the start and end of the season. The highest point, however, is not the altitude of the villages: a ski area can peak at 3,000 m while its villages sit at 1,800 m, and the orientation of the runs also affects snow quality.

3 entries in this ranking are ski areas grouping several resorts linked by runs (Tignes – Val d'Isère, Paradiski, Les Trois Vallées…). They are counted once, using their highest point.

Frequently asked questions

Where do you ski highest in the French Northern Alps?

According to SnowFinder data, Chamonix is the ski area with the highest peak in the French Northern Alps: 3,842 m.

Which ski areas should I choose for snow that lasts longer?

The highest ski areas in the ranking (Chamonix, Les Deux Alpes, Tignes – Val d'Isère) are generally the most reliable at the start and end of the season, but snow cover also depends on the weather of the year.

Does a high summit guarantee snow everywhere?

No. The highest point shows how high the ski area goes, not where the villages or every run are. Check real-time snow conditions before you set off.

Conclusion

In short, Chamonix takes first place in this ranking for the French Northern Alps (3,842 m), and La Norma closes the top 10 (2,750 m). But altitude is only one criterion among others: atmosphere, ski area size, accommodation and your budget matter just as much when choosing.
