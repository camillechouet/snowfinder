Which ski pass to choose in 2026-2027: day, stay, season, beginner or large ski area?

✍️ SnowFinder Editorial Team📅 16 November 2026⏱ 6 min

Depending on your level, the length of your stay and the areas you want to ski, the right pass isn't the same. With 2026-2027 price examples published at Méribel, in the Trois Vallées, in the Portes du Soleil, at La Plagne, Tignes, Val Cenis and Gérardmer, here are the key points for choosing.

Key points: In Méribel, in peak season 2026-2027, the adult day pass costs 71.20 € with the Vallée de Méribel pass and 84.20 € with the 3 Vallées pass. · On Saturdays, the adult 3 Vallées day pass gets 20% off, online only, if ordered by midnight on the Friday at the latest. · The Portes du Soleil season pass costs 1,108 € until 30 November 2026, then 1,303 €. · Val Cenis offers six free drag lifts from 13 December 2026 to 16 April 2027.

Single-valley pass or linked ski area pass?

Méribel is part of the Trois Vallées ski area, which also includes Courchevel, Val Thorens, Les Menuires, Saint-Martin-de-Belleville, La Tania and Orelle. In peak season 2026-2027, from 19 December to early April, the Vallée de Méribel pass costs 71.20 € per day and 356 € for 6 days for an adult. The 3 Vallées pass comes to 84.20 € per day and 421 € for 6 days.

The price difference reflects the area covered. If you plan to stay on the runs of your valley, the local pass may be enough. If you want to visit the other resorts in the area, you will need the linked ski area pass. Think about what you want to do before choosing.

Day, stay or Saturday: when is it cheaper?

In the Trois Vallées, the adult day pass gets 20% off every Saturday of the winter. This discount applies to the 84.20 € rate in force from 19 December 2026 to 10 April 2027. It is reserved for online purchases, and the order must be placed by midnight on the Friday at the latest.

Some resorts sell their passes on a rechargeable card. At Gérardmer, this magnetic card costs 2 €, has no time limit and can be topped up online. Keep it from one season to the next to avoid buying a new one.

First steps on skis: free lifts

For a first taster, some resorts open their learning lifts for free. At Tignes, the Ski Start 1 and 2 zones are free. Zone 3 requires a Tignes day pass, or a Tignes – Val d'Isère pass for two days or more.

At Val Cenis, six free drag lifts are spread across three villages, from 13 December 2026 to 16 April 2027, every day from 9 h to 16 h 30. These lifts are free, but lessons remain paid: budget for them separately.

Free access by age: check the conditions

Age-based free access depends on each resort and may exclude some passes. At Gérardmer, for the 2026-2027 season, all passes are free for children under 5 and adults over 75, except the season pass and multi-day passes. Read these conditions before buying for the whole family.

Season pass: from how many days does it pay off?

To work out whether a season pass is worth it, compare its price with the day rate. At La Plagne it costs 759 €, the price of 11 full-price days at 72.00 €. Beyond that, each extra day is, in a way, free.

The purchase date matters too. The Portes du Soleil season pass costs 1,108 € until 30 November 2026, then 1,303 €. If you are sure you will ski enough days, buying before the deadline saves you money.

Our tips

  • Before buying a linked large ski area pass, ask yourself whether you really plan to ski in the other resorts. If not, your valley pass may be enough.
  • If you are a beginner, check whether the resort offers free learning lifts, and budget for lessons separately.
  • Before taking a season pass, estimate the number of days you will ski and compare with the day rate.
  • Keep your rechargeable card if the resort uses one, so you can top up your pass online.
  • To follow the snowpack: check our Snow conditions page, which brings together conditions resort by resort.

Méribel▾

1,100 – 2,952 m · 150 km of pistes · 43 lifts · day pass 69 €

826288

Méribel lies at the heart of the Trois Vallées (600 km) at 1,100–2,952 m. Its uniform architecture in Savoyard wood and stone, required since its founding in 1938, gives it a rare character. It hosted the women's events at the 1992 Albertville Olympic Games. Its own 150 km ski area (70 runs) offers 85% of terrain above 1,800 m.

Tignes▾

1,550 – 3,456 m · 150 km of runs · 33 lifts · day lift pass 70 €

6382016

Tignes was born in the 1950s after the filling of the Chevril dam. Together with Val d'Isère, it forms the Tignes-Val d'Isère ski area (300 km). Its Grande Motte glacier (3,456 m), open almost all year round, makes it a world reference. A car-free resort, hugely popular with snowboarders and freeriders, with one of Europe's best snowparks.

Val Cenis▾

1,400 – 2,800 m · 125 km of pistes · 29 lifts · day pass 50 €

1521245

Val Cenis is a Haute-Maurienne resort at 1,400 m, grouping several villages including Lanslebourg and Lanslevillard. Its 125 km ski area is one of the sunniest in Savoie. Gateway to the Vanoise and its national park, it offers varied skiing in a magnificent high-mountain setting.

Gérardmer▾

750 – 1,140 m · 40 km of pistes · 18 lifts · day pass 36 €

Gérardmer is the best-known resort in the Vosges, at 660 m beside its famous lake. Its 40 km of runs are the best equipped in the range. Each January the town hosts the Festival du Film Fantastique, one of the largest in Europe. A unique ski, nature and culture destination.
