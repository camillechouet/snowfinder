Isola 2000: the only resort in France where you can ski with a view of the Mediterranean

✍️ SnowFinder Editorial📅 25 July 2026⏱ 6 min read

There are resorts you choose for their ski area, others for their atmosphere, and then there is Isola 2000, which you choose for an almost improbable fact: it is the ski resort closest to the Mediterranean in Europe. From certain summits of the ski area, on clear days, you can make out the sea. Not a vague postcard, but a real blue line on the horizon, some fifty kilometres away as the crow flies.

Mornings on the slopes, afternoons on the Promenade

This is the claim the resort makes itself, and it is no empty marketing slogan: skiing in the morning at Isola 2000 and ending the day with your feet in the water in Nice is an experience you can't find anywhere else in France. Allow around 1h15 to 1h30 of driving between the top of the runs and the Promenade des Anglais. It is easily doable in a single day for a disciplined weekend skier.

The resort itself plays along: every year in April, a challenge is organised to combine skiing on the slopes of Isola in the morning with water activities on the Niçois coast in the afternoon. A very concrete way of celebrating what makes the place unique.

💡
Did you know? Isola 2000 paradoxically owes its snow reliability to its proximity to the sea. The microclimate generated by the Gulf of Genoa brings, during certain north-westerly flows, snowfalls that are as rare as they are heavy. As a result, the resort was the snowiest in France during the winters of 2010-2011 and 2014-2015, despite its position at the far south of the country.

A resort born from nothing, in 1971

Before 1971, the site of Isola 2000 was just a quiet alpine pasture at 2,000 m above sea level on the Chastillon plateau. It was the British Olympic skier Peter Boumphrey who had the idea for the project, financed by the Bernard Sunley investment fund. The site was no accident: its aspect and microclimate already guaranteed, on paper, reliable snow despite the southern latitude.

The idea for the project is credited to Michel Renaud, but it was town planner Gérald Hanning, a former collaborator of Le Corbusier, who drew up the overall layout of the resort from 1970 with architect Henri Béri. Their approach was openly modernist architecture, in terraces following the natural slope of the land, in contrast to the 'pseudo-chalets' fashionable elsewhere in the Alps. The Front de Neige, the first complex built, is today recognised as a notable architectural testimony of the 1970s, and has been labelled 'remarkable building' by the Ministry of Culture.

A ski area built for every level

With 120 km of runs and 20 lifts, Isola 2000 is anything but a tiny, obscure ski area. Seven green runs and twenty-one blue make it a particularly reassuring learning ground for families, while the eleven red and four black runs keep more experienced skiers seriously busy. The 'Mercantour' lift pass, which includes the neighbouring resort of Auron, takes the total to 255 km.

The Tour de France didn't miss the occasion either: the resort hosted a stage finish in 1993 and again in 2024, proof that the road climbing up here is also worth the detour in summer.

Practical information

Access 90 km / 1h15-1h30 from Nice (M6202 bis then D97), same route from Monaco via the A8
Budget adult day lift pass around €35-44 depending on the period, dynamic pricing from online booking
Atmosphere family-friendly, sunny resort, wide and reassuring runs; functional rather than picturesque architecture
Best for families, beginners, and anyone who wants to prove you can ski and see the sea on the same day

Isola 2000 doesn't have the fame of Val d'Isère or Chamonix, and that's almost a good thing: fewer crowds, gentler prices, and a claim no other French resort can really make. If someone tells you one day that you can't ski and sunbathe on the same weekend in France, send them this article.
