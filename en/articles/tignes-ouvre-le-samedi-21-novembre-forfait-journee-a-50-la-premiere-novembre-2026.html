Tignes opens on Saturday 21 November: day lift pass at 50 € for the first week

✍️ SnowFinder editorial team📅 11 November 2026⏱ 4 min

Tignes kicks off the winter on Saturday 21 November on the Grande Motte glacier, with a single rate of 50 € a day throughout the first week of opening. The link with Val d'Isère is due to follow from Saturday 28 November. All these dates depend on snow conditions.

Key points: Opening on Saturday 21 November, with lifts running from 9am · Day lift pass at €50 for everyone from Saturday 21 to Friday 27 November · Link with Val d'Isère planned from Saturday 28 November · Tignes – Val d'Isère day pass at €67 in preview, from Saturday 28 November to Friday 11 December

Where and when to ski in Tignes from opening day?

Tignes' season opens on Saturday 21 November, with the lifts running from 9 a.m. The first skiers will be welcomed on the Grande Motte glacier, from where you can reach the ski area up to 3,456 metres above sea level. These dates depend on snow conditions. It is also the first winter for the company ALTTA: created in January 2025 by the municipalities of Tignes and Sainte-Foy-Tarentaise, it has operated these two resorts since 1 June 2026.

How much does the lift pass cost in the first week?

Le samedi 21 et le dimanche 22 novembre, le forfait d'une journée coûte 50 € quel que soit l'âge et permet de skier de 9 heures à 16 h 30. Ce week-end de lancement, appelé Opening, accueille aussi la Yéti Race. Le même tarif unique de 50 € la journée reste en vigueur pendant la première semaine d'ouverture, du lundi 23 au vendredi 27 novembre.

When will Tignes be linked to Val d'Isère?

The link with Val d'Isère is planned from Saturday 28 November, again depending on snow conditions. The preview period, from Saturday 28 November to Friday 11 December, is one of the cheapest times of the season: the Tignes – Val d'Isère day pass is reduced to 67 €. After that, the ski area should be fully open from Saturday 12 December to Friday 23 April 2027, and the season ends on Sunday 2 May.

Our tips

  • Check which runs and lifts are open just before you leave, as opening depends on snow conditions.
  • If your stay starts before the link opens and continues afterwards, compare the Tignes-only lift pass with the Tignes – Val d'Isère pass according to the days you ski.
  • At the very start of the season, most skiing is on the high-altitude glacier, so pack warm clothing and good sun protection.
  • To follow the snowpack: check our Snow conditions page, which brings together conditions resort by resort.

Tignes▾

1,550 – 3,456 m · 150 km of runs · 33 lifts · day lift pass 70 €

6382016

Tignes was born in the 1950s after the filling of the Chevril dam. Together with Val d'Isère, it forms the Tignes-Val d'Isère ski area (300 km). Its Grande Motte glacier (3,456 m), open almost all year round, makes it a world reference. A car-free resort, hugely popular with snowboarders and freeriders, with one of Europe's best snowparks.

Val d'Isère▾

1,850 – 3,456 m · 150 km of runs · 38 lifts · day lift pass 70 €

15332211

Val d'Isère is one of the most legendary resorts in the Alps, perched at 1,850 m in the Haute-Tarentaise. Together with Tignes, it forms the Tignes-Val d'Isère ski area (300 km), which hosted the 1992 Olympic Games. The Pisaillas glacier rises to 3,456 m, guaranteeing snow from November to May. A paradise for technical skiing and freeride, the resort also offers a lively nightlife and high-end accommodation.
